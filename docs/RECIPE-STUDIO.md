# Sous-chef: chat, live voice, and camera

The sous-chef is one assistant for anything the app does. The floating button on every screen opens it, as do **Ask** on a recipe page and the sparkle button in cooking mode; **Create a recipe → Speak / Scan / Chat** opens it in live voice, the camera, or text, ready to write a new recipe: it says so ("Tell me your recipe", what to include, **New recipe** over the message box) and tells the assistant, which asks for the recipe and saves it as a new draft. It writes in the open conversation while nothing has been said there or it is already writing a recipe without a draft, and otherwise starts a conversation of its own; the conversation stays about the new recipe, however it's reopened, until the recipe is published. A search's **Create "…" with the sous-chef** opens it the same way, with its request as the first message. The home's **Not sure what to make?** card and its ideas, a search's **Create "…" with the sous-chef**, and the planner's and pantry's cards open it too, sending their prompt as the first message. Typed messages, live speech, and photos share one history, draft, and background image/video pipeline.

A recipe's twists, ingredient swaps and nutrition estimate, and the editor's photo and clip generation, are one-off requests (`/v1/ai/jobs`) rather than turns of the conversation, so an adapted recipe keeps its link to the original. Their sheet shares the conversation's look: the same header, message bubbles, thinking dots and message box (`src/components/ChatBubbles.tsx`). While a reply is on its way the sous-chef shows three dots in a bubble, and the reply grows out of that bubble when it arrives.

## What it can do

- Find recipes (text, ingredients, cuisine, tag, cook, time) and cooks, read reviews, and show tappable recipe cards in the reply.
- Read any published recipe or the user's own; answer cooking questions, swaps, and scaling.
- Save and unsave recipes, create and delete cookbooks, follow and unfollow cooks, review and report recipes.
- Plan meals, add groceries or a whole recipe's ingredients, check items off, keep the pantry, and remove plan/grocery/pantry entries.
- Create recipes (below), and edit, publish, unpublish, or delete the user's existing recipes. Edits keep the recipe's photos; it can also make a new AI cover photo or video on request.
- Update the user's name, bio, diets, and allergies.
- Act on screen: open a recipe, start cooking mode at a step, move between steps, start the step timer, open a page, or change the interface language.

It cannot change email, password, or sign-in, or delete the account; those stay in My kitchen. Several steps run in one turn ("plan three quick dinners and add their groceries"). After each reply the app refreshes the planner, lists, cookbooks, and the open recipe. Every action goes through the same HTTP handlers as the app (an in-process router that registers only the assistant's routes), so validation and ownership checks are identical.

**What it sees.** While the sheet is open (or minimized), the app reports the open page, the open recipe, the cooking-mode step, the device time zone, the interface language, and, from Create a recipe, that it's there to write a new recipe (`PUT /:id/context`; the assistant sees `screen.purpose: "newRecipe"`, and a voice call opened that way starts by asking for the recipe). The server allowlists the page, shows the recipe only if the owner may see it, validates the time zone, and keeps the language only if it is one of the app's nine, so "this recipe", "next step", and "tomorrow" resolve in the user's own day, not the server's. The language picks the reply the server writes itself when a turn is cut off (below); English is the fallback. Each turn sees the conversation's opening request and its most recent messages, not the whole history (see [Backend and persistence](#backend-and-persistence)). It also sees whether it came from a live call or the chat, the app language, the member's units when they chose them, what their plan has left for the rolling month (when plans apply, so it doesn't offer a photo or video that would be refused), a glance at their week (meals planned for the next seven days, the first 30 groceries still to buy, pantry items expiring within three days; `get_kitchen` has the rest, with ids), and the recipe cards its recent replies showed, so "the second one" resolves.

**What else it can do.** Besides finding, planning, shopping and editing, it reads a recipe page the member shares (`import_recipe`: the page's schema.org recipe, or its text to transcribe, without its photos; nothing is saved until it saves a draft, which credits the page only when the member shared that address in the conversation), puts a range of planned days on the grocery list in one step (`add_plan_groceries`), and records that the member cooked a recipe, with their note (`mark_cooked`, with Undo restoring what was recorded before). A member's own private recipes can be planned and shopped, from the app or the sous-chef.

**Confirmation.** Deleting a recipe, publishing one, changing a published recipe, posting a review, reporting a recipe, changing the member's profile (name, bio, diets, allergies), and deleting a cookbook or more than three plan, grocery or pantry items return a server-written description instead of acting ("Update your published recipe “Lasagna”: servings 4 → 6; adds basil."). The app stores the exact action and shows its description to the model in the next turn (`awaitingConfirmation`), so a yes runs what was described, not a re-generated version of it. The app shows **Yes** and **No** under the question (**Publish** and **Not now** after a read-back); a tap is carried out by the server itself before the model replies, so the decision never rests on the model's reading of the answer. A typed or spoken yes still works through `confirm_action`, which runs it only after a user message that follows the reply that asked (in a live call, after the assistant began reading it out, so words said while it was still working don't count) and only as the turn's first tool call: anything the model does first (reading a recipe or reviews, whose text could look like agreement) drops the proposal, and so does a turn that does something else. A draft created in the conversation keeps its own read-back gate, and `publish_draft` is likewise accepted only as a turn's first tool call. A turn that fails or is cut off drops both a pending proposal and a read-back approval, since the user never saw its answer. Once a conversation's recipe is published (including one of the user's recipes the sous-chef adopted to make media for), `save_recipe_draft` refuses to replace it; changes go through `edit_recipe` and its confirmation, and an adopted recipe keeps its own photos and its human-made label.

**What it did.** Each reply lists what its turn changed as receipts under it ("Added 6 items to your groceries", "Planned Lemon toast"), with **View** (the recipe, or the page that lists it) and, where the server can take it back, **Undo**: groceries added or merged, a planned meal (the slot gets back what it held), a pantry item, a save or follow, a new cookbook, a check-off. Deletions, edits, drafts, publishing and profile changes show a receipt without Undo. A turn that changed nothing doesn't refresh the app's lists. While a reply takes a while, a caption under the thinking dots says what it is doing and for how long, with **Stop**. A failed or stopped reply shows once, in red, with **Try again** for the message before it. On a phone, a typed chat steps aside into its pill when the sous-chef opens something, showing its reply as a toast rather than closing before it was read.

**Live voice while browsing.** Opening a recipe or cooking mode from a live conversation minimizes the sheet to a pill above the tab bar, and the conversation keeps listening; the pill returns to the chat or ends the call. A typed chat closes instead.

**In a live call.** The voice model starts with a brief of what the user has open, appended to its instructions as app data: the page, or the open recipe with its ingredients and steps and the cooking-mode step, plus the member's diets and allergies, their local time, the app language, and why the conversation was opened. It answers questions about that recipe and step itself ("how much flour?", "what does this step say?") without a backend turn; anything that changes the screen still goes to the backend. When the user opens another recipe or page, moves to another step, or the open recipe is edited, the server tells the call as session-wide context (`session.instructions.append` with no delegation), once the assistant has stopped speaking, since an instruction can cut its speech short. A reply reaches the call the moment its turn settles: in the same process directly, and on another replica through `NOTIFY` (`reply:<conversation>` on `savorly_work`), rather than at the monitor's two-second check. In a call, a turn that only drives the screen ends after one model round when the model writes its reply alongside the screen action (the agent sees `channel: "voice"`). What the server says itself in a call (pausing the microphone, media ready or unfinished, a failed or stopped turn) is in the app's language. The voice bar says whether the sous-chef is speaking, working on what was asked (and on what), or listening. When the call will use up the plan's voice time, its clock counts down what is left, and the call ends at the limit with the plan message rather than renewing into a refusal. A call that drops shows **Resume voice**.

## Recipe flow

- **Talk:** the **Talk** button in the composer (or **Speak it**) requests microphone permission and starts a GPT-Live conversation. Dictate naturally, including pauses and corrections. Say “make it” or “that's everything” when finished. The assistant asks essential missing details aloud, one at a time. Say “read it back,” “publish,” “retry the media,” or “save and finish.” Publishing requires a review and explicit affirmation of the current revision.
- **Photo:** use the in-app camera (preview, switch camera, capture, retake) or upload an image. The app normalizes the photo to JPEG, scales large images to 2048px width, and submits it automatically. The server keeps photos at most 1600 px on their longest side: larger JPEG, PNG, and WebP photos are re-encoded as JPEG (quality 85, turned upright per their EXIF orientation, transparency on white), which keeps cookbook text legible while bounding what each turn costs. The vision model reads the recipe, asks about illegible or missing details in chat, and creates the same draft/media bundle. You can switch to voice to answer.
- **Chat:** ask for anything, or describe a recipe or rough idea, answer questions, request changes, and review/publish conversationally. A single message supports up to 24,000 characters. A conversation can contain multiple messages and images.
- The recipe is saved privately before media is queued. The cover image and short Seedance dish video run independently. Their results attach automatically. Media continues after closing the sheet or microphone. The video is a generated dish clip, not a verified video of every cooking step.
- A conversation active in the last six hours resumes when the sheet opens; older ones are in the history row, and **New chat** starts fresh. One conversation holds one draft at a time; starting a different recipe waits until the current draft's media has attached.
- Recent conversations resume across devices. Corrections invalidate publication approval and old media revisions; manually editing the recipe also prevents an older AI image/video from replacing those edits. Failed media can be retried explicitly, with a new generation charge. Photos and transcripts are not automatically published.

## Configure

Add to the repository `.env` (never an Expo public environment variable):

```dotenv
OPENAI_API_KEY=...
OPENAI_PROJECT_ID=
OPENAI_LIVE_MODEL=gpt-live-1
OPENAI_LIVE_VOICE=gleam
OPENROUTER_API_KEY=...
OPENROUTER_AGENT_MODEL=openai/gpt-6-luna
OPENROUTER_IMAGE_MODEL=openai/gpt-image-2.5-flare
REAPI_API_KEY=...
AI_DAILY_BUDGET_USD=10
S3_SOURCE_BUCKET=savorly-sources
```

The OpenAI project must have access to `gpt-live-1`. `OPENAI_LIVE_MODEL` selects the direct OpenAI audio model; `OPENROUTER_AGENT_MODEL` selects the backend reasoning/vision agent for both text chat and Live delegations. The legacy `OPENROUTER_VOICE_MODEL` name remains a fallback for older deployments but does not select audio. GPT-6 Luna runs with low reasoning effort and latency-prioritized routing; tool round trips preserve the provider's required reasoning metadata. Images use GPT Image 2.5 Flare through OpenRouter; videos use the existing Seedance 2.5 reAPI adapter. Cooking step narration uses ElevenLabs v4 Turbo through the same OpenRouter account and key, separately from the live conversation audio. The model/voice selection is server controlled and the selected models are visible through `/v1/capabilities`. See [all model choices and prices](PROVIDERS.md).

GPT-Live currently costs $0.05 per connected minute, including listening and waiting; recipe reasoning, image generation, and video generation are additional. Each plan includes live voice minutes per rolling 30 days (see [pricing](PRICING.md)). The application reserves up to 30-minute segments against the member's allowance and AI spend ceiling; a smaller remaining allowance reserves only that time. A session then bills what was actually used whenever that can be known, and holds its full reservation only while usage may still be accruing where the server can't see it:

- **Normal end:** the provider's final usage from `session.closed` (the server sends `session.close` and waits for it).
- **Failed handshake:** nothing, if the provider never created a session; the 15-second minimum if it did (for example, the sideband couldn't attach).
- **Server stopping** (a deploy or restart): the server closes the session at the provider, so nothing accrues unseen, waits up to three seconds for the final usage, and otherwise bills the connected time it observed (at least 15 seconds). The worker waits for this before the database pool closes.
- **Sideband dropped:** the server re-attaches to the same session (three tries, about four seconds). If it can't, it hangs up over REST (`POST /live/sessions/{id}/hangup`) and bills the observed time. If it can do neither, the session keeps its reservation and the app is told the call was interrupted, so it hangs up its side; the stale sweep then finalizes the session.
- **Stale sweep:** every minute (and when the same member starts a new call), any session still unfinalized whose heartbeat is more than 90 seconds old (its server crashed, was replaced, or lost it) is marked interrupted and billed the connected time last recorded, at least 15 seconds. It stops holding the member's voice slot, blocking deletion, and counting as live in the admin console.

Provider hard spending caps remain necessary because estimated costs are not invoices. Three minutes without user speech pauses the connection. A member has one voice slot: ending a call (`DELETE /voice/:session`, also sent when a 30-minute lease renews) frees it at once (the session shows `closing` while it says its last words), so the renewal's new session starts straight away; the app waits for that close, up to three seconds, before renewing. There is no 60-second recording limit or silent transcript truncation; session renewal and explicit resume retain history. A reconnect can briefly interrupt capture, and the UI indicates reconnection. Plan allowances still apply.

For local development, restart SeaweedFS after updating `infra/seaweedfs/s3.json`. The authenticated app creates `savorly-sources` on first import. Anonymous access to this bucket is denied. Public recipe covers/videos remain in `savorly-media`.

For R2, provision a **separate private bucket** named by `S3_SOURCE_BUCKET`, leave `r2.dev` disabled, and do not attach a public custom domain. Grant the backend credentials access to both buckets. The source bucket is not automatically provisioned in production. Source photos are read through authenticated S3 and sent to the vision model as data URLs, so local SeaweedFS does not need to be publicly reachable. Do not make the source bucket public.

Restart the API after changing its environment. Migrations `004_voice_recipes.sql` through `006_studio_confirmation.sql`, `009_assistant_cards.sql`, `010_assistant_context.sql`, `019_ai_worker.sql`, `020_studio_retention.sql`, `024_sous_chef_answers.sql`, and `025_sous_chef_receipts.sql` run automatically. No provider calls are made during migrations or ordinary browsing.

## Expo and Cloudflare

Web uses browser WebRTC for bidirectional audio. `react-native-webrtc` provides the native adapter; native voice requires a new development/production build, not Expo Go. Camera and image import use Expo Camera, ImagePicker, and ImageManipulator. Native audio mode enables recording/speaker playback and avoids background recording. An active voice conversation keeps the screen awake where supported, so device auto-lock does not interrupt hands-free dictation. Closing the sheet, switching modes, or backgrounding the app stops microphone tracks and closes the provider session. Browser autoplay restrictions expose an “Enable assistant audio” button.

Configure `EXPO_PUBLIC_API_URL` for physical devices to reach the API. Run `eas build --profile development` after native dependencies/config plugins change. The web app must be on HTTPS or localhost. Cloudflare now allows same-origin camera and microphone access and runs the Worker for static responses as well as API requests, so the headers are applied consistently.

## Backend and persistence

- `/v1/recipe-studio`: list/create conversations. Creation accepts a UUID `Idempotency-Key`. A member keeps up to 200 conversations. Creating one past that erases the oldest that have been idle for 30 days with nothing running and no call open, exactly as deleting them would; the app lists only the 20 most recent. Only a member whose 200 conversations are all in use gets `409`.
- `/:id`: read/delete an owned conversation. End voice before deleting, including a call that is still closing. See [Deleting and retention](#deleting-and-retention).
- `/:id/events?after=…`: paginated transcript events, 1,000 per request, monotonically increasing cursor. The app drains pages without discarding earlier transcript text. Assistant events carry `recipes` (cards it chose to show, filtered to recipes the owner can see whose cooks aren't suspended) and `actions` (screen actions, run only for replies that arrive while the sheet is open).
- `/:id/context` (PUT): the page, open recipe, cooking step, IANA time zone, interface language (`en`, `fr`, `de`, `ru`, `es`, `it`, `pt`, `zh`, `ja`; optional) and purpose (`recipe` from Create a recipe; optional) the assistant should assume. Conversations list the purpose they were last opened for.
- `/:id/messages`: enqueue text plus optional owned image IDs; requires `Idempotency-Key`. Replayed keys cannot create duplicate work or carry different input.
- `/:id/images`: authenticated multipart photo upload; returns an opaque source ID, not a public URL. JPEG/PNG/WebP, at most 10 MB each, four attachments per message, twenty per conversation, and 200 per member across conversations (`400` beyond that). Photos over 1600 px are stored downscaled (see Photo above).
- `/:id/voice`: exchange a WebRTC SDP offer for an answer. Only the server uses the OpenAI key. A server sideband attaches before the answer is returned, persists transcript fragments, and accepts model delegations.
- `/voice/:session`: request a graceful close of an owned voice connection. It frees the member's voice slot at once.

The sideband ignores reflected audio payloads. OpenAI session storage is disabled (`store:false`); Savorly persists text transcripts, not raw microphone recordings. Provider retention policies still apply. Source deletion uses a durable outbox so deleting a conversation or account also removes its private source objects from storage; the worker must be running for cleanup to finish.

Each message or voice delegation runs one bounded tool loop (at most six model calls, the last forced to answer in text). A turn doesn't resend the whole conversation and every photo. It sends the opening request (in full) and the newest messages that fit in 40 messages and 24,000 bytes, always including the latest; consecutive transcript fragments from one side count as one message, and `earlierMessagesOmitted` tells the model how many were left out. It attaches photos only from the user's last two messages, at most four, re-reading older stored photos at 1600 px. Older context lives in the draft. The reservation is computed from that same window when the message is accepted: each byte counts as a token, to allow for long multilingual dictation, and each photo as 8,192 tokens, plus instructions, tool schemas and output, resent on every round. After a completed turn it is settled to the cost OpenRouter reports (`usage.cost`); a failed turn keeps its reservation, unless it failed before reaching the model, which costs nothing and doesn't count against the allowance. A saved draft's reply says which media is actually on its way: a plan without video, or a used-up photo allowance, gets no promise of one. Settlement runs on its own short deadline, so a turn that hits its three-minute limit, or a worker that is stopping, still records its answer straight away rather than leaving the conversation busy until the five-minute sweep. A failed turn that already changed data says so, so the next turn checks before repeating.

The application, not the model, enforces ownership, validates recipe shape, writes SQL, checks current revisions, gates publishing, reserves cost, and enqueues media. Delegations have stable IDs and database uniqueness. The studio worker runs up to eight turns per replica at once, still one per conversation (the claim skips conversations with a turn running, and a unique index holds across replicas); stopping cancels turns in flight and each still records its outcome. The media worker uses bounded parallel execution, `SKIP LOCKED` claims, and durable states. Asking for media that is already being made neither orders nor reserves it twice, and media for an existing recipe is checked against the plan and budgets before the conversation adopts that recipe. Interrupted ambiguous paid submissions are not blindly replayed. Idle worker loops back off to one look every 30 seconds. Enqueueing wakes them at once, in-process and on other replicas through Postgres `LISTEN`/`NOTIFY` (channel `savorly_work`, on one dedicated pooled connection that reconnects with backoff; behind a transaction-mode pooler notifications don't arrive and the 30-second look applies). Recovery sweeps run every minute: turns stuck over five minutes (answered in the conversation's language), stale voice sessions, media left unqueued by a crash between draft creation and media enqueueing, and private photo deletions. Deleted conversations are purged hourly.

### Deleting and retention

Deleting a conversation (`DELETE /v1/recipe-studio/:id`) erases its content at once: transcripts, private photos (their objects are queued for removal from the bucket), links to its draft and media, the assistant's replies, any pending action, and what the app reported having open. Recipes made in it stay as they are. A contentless shell stays behind with the conversation's turns and voice sessions, because they carry the member's spend and allowance use for the rolling 30 days. Deleting history therefore doesn't give allowances back. A turn still waiting is cancelled and releases its reservation, since it never reached the provider. A turn already with the provider keeps its reservation, and its answer is not written back. Deleted conversations disappear from lists and reads, and messages, context, photos and voice for them answer `404`. The worker purges a shell 35 days after deletion, once its records have left every 30-day window.

## Verification and limits

Automated checks cover structured clarification/build, media attachment, stale revision rejection, manual-edit protection, publish review gating, long transcript preservation, message idempotency, owned photo references, budget exhaustion without losing a recipe, recovery, private SeaweedFS source reads/deletion, and the Live WebRTC/sideband contract. They also cover:

- the history window and its reservation against the Free spend cap;
- photo downscaling and orientation;
- confirmation as a turn's first act, and reporting held for a yes;
- failed and timed-out turns dropping proposals and read-backs;
- malformed tool calls and provider errors mid-turn;
- parallel turns, waking on new work, and a clean stop;
- voice billing on handshake failures, a server stop, a dropped sideband (rejoined, hung up, or lost and swept), and a graceful close, with a fake GPT-Live;
- what a turn knows of the member's week, units, plan and shown cards; reading a shared page into a credited draft (and refusing a credit for a page they never shared); marking a recipe cooked and shopping a planned week, each with Undo;
- what a call is told the user has open, the quiet updates as they move (never describing a recipe they may not see), replies said as soon as their turn settles, the server's own words in the app language, and one-round screen turns;
- the renewal slot;
- conversation and photo caps;
- deletion keeping usage and spend while erasing content, and the 35-day purge.

Browser tests use mocked provider responses and synthetic camera/microphone devices; they exercise real Expo camera capture/retake and JPEG upload handling.

No funded OpenAI, OpenRouter, or reAPI requests have been made. No physical iOS/Android microphone/camera test, native binary build, or cloud deployment is implied by JavaScript bundle exports. Verify these with the configured accounts and a device before launch. Full-duplex voice quality, real provider event ordering, model extraction accuracy, and latency need that live validation. There is no claim of unlimited provider context or zero-gap reconnection.
