# Whole-meal timeline

Pick two to four recipes and a time to eat, and Savorly turns them into one timed plan so everything is ready together, then guides it in one cooking mode, hands-free if the cook likes. Holiday dinners and a weeknight main with two sides are where cooks get lost: which goes in first, when the oven is free, what can wait. The plan is worked out on the server without AI, from how each step spends its time, and it gets more accurate as people cook: once 5 cooks have measured a step, their time replaces the written one.

Code: `services/api/internal/app/meal_timeline.go` (the scheduler, `POST /meals/timeline`, `POST /meals/cook-sessions`), `step_timing.go` and `step_timing_lexicon.go` (reading each step's time from its words), `step_timing_model.go` and `internal/ai/timing.go` (the cheap model's estimates), migration `041_meal_timeline.sql`. App: `src/lib/mealTimeline.ts` (the plan, cooking through it, the sessions), `src/components/CookTogether.tsx` (choosing, "Eat at", the list), `src/components/MealCookMode.tsx` (cooking mode across the dishes), `src/components/StepTimingEditor.tsx` (the editor's line).

## What cooks see

- **Cook together.** The planner offers it for a day with two or more meals the cook can open (a phone under the day's meals, a desktop at the end of the day's row), and Saved for a few of the recipes on show. The sheet opens on the plan for a day's meals, or on a choice of 2 to 4 saved recipes.
- **Eat at.** Dinner at 7:00 unless the day's meals are lunch (12:30) or breakfast (8:30); when that's too soon to cook, an hour and a half from now, on a quarter hour. Two buttons move it by 15 minutes, and the plan is asked for again. A plan that would have started already says so in one line, with a link to the time that starts now.
- **One list across the dishes.** Every step at its start time, marked by its dish's colour dot: "5:20 PM · Roast chicken: Roast · 75 min" (a step that waits shows how long it takes in all), with the stretches of free time between hands-on work ("26 minutes free"). The dishes are named once above it, with the same dots. A preheat reads "Preheat the oven to 220 °C" (or 425 °F where the cook reads imperial amounts or lives in the US, to the nearest 25 °F). "Some times are estimates." is one quiet line when they are.
- **Warnings** are quiet lines above the list, never a step left out: two dishes needing the oven at different heats ("Roast chicken and Crispy potatoes need the oven at once, at 220 °C and 180 °C."), more burners than the kitchen has ("3 burners at once around 6:45 PM, but your kitchen has 2."), and too much to do at once ("Too much at once around 6:30 PM: Green beans waits 20 minutes.").
- **The kitchen** is one quiet line ("4 burners · 1 oven") whose button opens two steppers: 1–12 burners and 1–3 ovens, kept on the device.
- **Cooking mode across all dishes.** The step to do now, with its dish, its place in the recipe and its planned time; what's next ("Next at 6:02 PM: Crispy potatoes: Parboil"); and a timer per dish side by side, each tapped to pause or run on. **Done** finishes a step's hands-on part: a step that then waits (baking, simmering, resting) starts its dish's timer, and the cook goes on with another dish; when a timer is up the dish's next step can start, and Savorly says so aloud and, when the cook is away, with a system alert. A step planned for later says "You have 20 minutes free first."; with nothing to do until a timer ends, the screen says that. Switching the oven on starts an "Oven heating" timer, and a dish's oven step waits for it to be on, not hot. **Back** takes the last step back, timer and all, and **I'm behind** moves everything not yet started later, so the step to do now starts now (by 5 minutes at least); dinner's time in the header moves with it.
- **Hands-free** works across the dishes ([hands-free cooking](HANDS-FREE.md#cooking-a-meal)).

## Step timing

A step can say how it spends its time, as `timing` on the step ([recipe shape](API.md#recipe-shape)): seconds that need the cook's hands (`active`) and seconds that need no attention (`wait`: baking, simmering, resting, chilling, rising), the oven's heat in °C while it runs (`oven`, 0 for none), the burners it takes (`burners`, each with its pot or pan), and `estimated` when a model or a reading of its words guessed it. A step that heats the oven and takes no time is preheating it.

- **The editor** has one quiet line under each step's timer: "Timing for cooking with other dishes", or what's set ("5 min hands-on · 30 min waiting · Oven 400 °F", "Estimated: …" for an estimate). It opens to four small fields and a link to clear them. Changing any of it makes it the cook's own; changing the words of a step whose timing was an estimate clears it, so it's estimated afresh.
- **Imports** fill in what a page's steps say in words, as estimates; recipe jobs (pages without recipe data, video captions) and the sous-chef's drafts ask the model for it in the same shape, held to what a step may have and marked as estimates, unless it's what the cook already gave that step.
- **Older apps and the sous-chef** save recipes without `timing`: like step photos, a step keeps the saved timing of the step with the same text. A recipe fix that changes a step's words clears its timing.
- **The server's estimate**, for every step its cook didn't time, is made when the recipe is saved and kept by step key in `recipes.step_timing`; writes that didn't read it (the seed, an older server) are caught up by a pass at start-up and every ten minutes (`RunStepTiming`), as is everything when the lexicon changes (`timingVersion`). It reads the step's title and words with a lexicon in the app's nine languages: times ("25 minutes", "1 h 30", "an hour and a half", "20–25 Minuten" as its longer end, "полчаса", "1時間半", "二十分钟", overnight as 8 hours, "per side" twice), oven heats ("200 °C", "400°F", "180 Grad", "200度", gas marks, French thermostats; below 90 °C it's the food's heat, not the oven's), the oven (the equipment lexicon's, plus "roast", "запекайте", and not "remove from the oven"), preheating, waiting ("bake", "simmer", "let rest", "chill", "a lid on"), constant attention, which makes a waiting step hands-on ("stirring constantly"), and the hob ("boil", "fry", "over medium heat", or a pot or pan the method names). A waiting step has a minute of hands at its start. An oven step without a heat takes the recipe's first one, else 180 °C.
- **Steps the words don't time** ("fry the onions until golden") get their share of what the recipe's minutes leave, between 3 and 20 minutes, as a guess. Once per version of a published recipe, the cheap translation model (GPT-6 Luna through OpenRouter) estimates its guessed steps from the whole recipe, at most `STEP_TIMING_RECIPES` (200) recipes a day, the most saved first, under an advisory lock. Each request reserves its cost against the shared free budget like a translation (`reserveShared`, `ai_usage` kind `step_timing`, no member pays; about a twentieth of a cent a recipe) and settles to what OpenRouter reports. What it answers is checked: only the steps asked about, within a day, an oven between 50 and 300 °C or none, and up to 2 burners. A request that reached the provider isn't sent again for that version, answered or not; editing a step asks about the new one only. Without an OpenRouter key, or with the day's cap or budget spent, guesses stay guesses.

## The scheduler

`scheduleMeal` places every step, backwards from the eating time, without AI:

1. **Each step's time** is its timing in whole minutes, or, once 5 cooks spent usable time on it (`recipe_step_stats`, [cook-tested recipes](COOK-TESTED.md)), their median: more or less waiting for a waiting step, all hands for one that isn't. A preheat keeps the timeline's own time.
2. **From the last step back**: of each dish's latest step not yet placed, the one whose hands-on part can end latest goes next, the longest dish first when they tie, so dishes finish together and hands-on work is laid out back to back from the meal. Each step ends as late as it can before the next step of its dish (the meal, for the last), as long as the cook's hands are on one step at a time and the kitchen holds it: oven heats within 10 °C are one heat, and a kitchen has as many heats at once as ovens, and its burners.
3. **What the kitchen can't do.** A step may go up to 15 minutes earlier than it would to keep the oven or the burners within what the kitchen has. Past that it stays where the cook's hands allow, and the plan warns, at the moment it happens: two oven heats at once (which dishes, at what heats), or more burners than the kitchen has (how many). Cooked food that waits more than 15 minutes because the cook was busy elsewhere (a waiting step's result, or a dish ready early) is a "too much at once" warning. One warning of each kind for the same dishes, the earliest.
4. **Preheating** goes ahead of the oven step after it in its dish, by the 15 minutes an oven takes to heat, wherever the recipe has it, and holds the oven at its heat meanwhile. A preheat with no oven step after it stays where it is and takes no time.
5. **Free time** is every stretch of 5 minutes or more with nothing hands-on to do, from the first step to the meal.

`meal_timeline_test.go` plans a roast with two sides (ready together at one heat, the potatoes roasting with the chicken, preheated a quarter of an hour ahead), a holiday dinner of four (the turkey out a little early so the potatoes roast at 220 °C while it rests, and the stuffing's 190 °C a warning; with two ovens, none), a weeknight main and two sides sharing the hob, three long simmers on a two-burner hob (a warning; four burners hold them; a short clash moves a step instead), four dishes finished by hand at once (staggered, with a warning for those that wait), preheats, cook-tested times, and 400 random meals in random kitchens, checking every step is placed once, in its dish's order, with the cook's hands on one thing at a time.

## Cooked together, counted per dish

Starting cooking mode records one cook session per dish (`POST /meals/cook-sessions`), linked by the meal (`cook_sessions.meal_id`), for the recipes that count: published, someone else's ([creator fund](CREATOR-FUND.md#verified-cooks)). Each dish's steps are entered when they're on screen and left when they're done, its timers started, paused and run out, and its furthest step sent as it goes, on its own session ([cook-tested recipes](COOK-TESTED.md)); a dish is finished with its last step. So each dish is a verified cook by the usual rules, counts for its recipe's cook time and creator's pay, and its steps' times feed its cook-tested numbers. Guests record nothing, members who keep their cooking out send no step events, and nothing waits on any of it. `GET /me/export` has each session's `mealId`.

## Not yet

- A meal's recipes are cooked as written, not in the reader's translation.
- The live sous-chef (the sparkle button) isn't in the meal's cooking mode; hands-free "Chef, …" questions are, about the dish on screen.
- A plan doesn't know how many dishes fit in the oven at one heat, or how long an oven takes to change heat.
- Cooking through a meal isn't kept if the app closes; the timeline can be planned again.
