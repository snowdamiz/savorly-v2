# Cook-tested recipes

Every run through cooking mode is evidence about a recipe: how many people really cooked it, how long each step really took, where cooks gave up, and what they asked at each step. Recipes show it ("Cook-tested · 214 cooks · usually 55 min (says 40)", "Most cooks needed 4 more minutes here"), their authors see where cooks get stuck, and the sous-chef uses it when it answers in cooking mode. Nobody else has this data: it comes only from people cooking in Savorly, and each cook improves the recipe for the next.

Code: `services/api/internal/app/cook_insights.go` (step events, questions, the nightly pass, the author's view), `services/api/internal/ai/questions.go` (grouping questions), migration `033_cook_tested.sql`. App: `src/lib/cookSessions.ts` (cooking mode's step events), `src/lib/cookInsights.ts` (the types and loading), `src/components/CookTested.tsx` (the recipe page's line), `src/components/CookInsights.tsx` (step notes, "Where cooks get stuck", the privacy switch).

## What cooks see

- **Cook-tested.** A recipe with 5 or more [verified cooks](CREATOR-FUND.md#verified-cooks) is cook-tested. Its page says so under the facts, with how many cooked it and how long they usually took, and the stated time beside it when theirs differs by at least 5 minutes or a tenth: "Cook-tested · 214 cooks · usually 55 min (says 40)". Below 5 it shows "Early: 3 cooks", with their usual time once 3 agree. Cards show a **Cook-tested** pill (after a warning about what the reader avoids, before the rating), and list rows a check.
- **Step notes.** A step says something only when its numbers do, from 5 sessions that spent time on it, and in as few words as the step already uses. Where cooks run over its timer by at least 2 minutes and a quarter of the timer, the step's timer line says how long they need ("Timer for 8 min in cooking mode · most cooks need about 12 min"), and cooking mode says it under the timer with **Set to 12 min**, which sets the timer to it before it starts. A step without a timer that takes 3 minutes or more says "Cooks usually spend about 12 minutes here".
- **Questions cooks keep asking**, under **Often asked here**: each a row that opens to its answer, once 3 different cooks asked it at that step in the last 90 days. Cooks see the two asked most at a step, never the cooks' own words (each is reworded), and, opened, whether the answer is the sous-chef's or the recipe's cook's.
- **Finished by 9 in 10 who started**, once 20 sessions are over (finished, or a day old). Tenths round down, so 96% is still 9 in 10, and "Finished by everyone who started" needs everyone.
- **In the sous-chef's answers.** In cooking mode, hands-free questions and the sous-chef get a line about the step the cook is on: how long other cooks' sessions usually took there and what they often ask, with the answer. So "Chef, how long do I simmer it?" can say "Cooks here usually needed 12 minutes, not 8."
- **In the meal timeline.** A step that 5 cooks measured takes their median time when it's planned with other dishes ([meal timeline](MEAL-TIMELINE.md)), and each dish of a meal cooked together sends its step events on its own session.
- **Explore and the feed.** Explore's filters have **Tested by cooks** (`cookTested=true`; the sous-chef's search has `cook_tested` too), and the home feed ranks a cook-tested recipe as if it had half as many saves again this week, and at least two more.

## What authors see

The author of a published recipe has **Where cooks get stuck** on its page. For every step it shows how long cooks usually take against the step's timer (highlighted where they run over it), how many of the cooks who reached it stopped there, and every question asked there by 3 cooks or more, with how many asked it. The author can **Approve** the sous-chef's answer (it then shows as theirs), write their own, or hide the question from cooks. Each step's pencil opens the editor.

A step is known by its text: a hash of its words, ignoring case and spacing (`cookStepKey`). Editing a step's text starts its numbers and questions afresh, while a new photo, a new title or steps added elsewhere keep them. A step repeated word for word shares its numbers.

## How it works

1. **Step events.** While cooking mode is open on someone else's published recipe, the app sends each step entered and left, and each timer started, paused and run out, on the cook session it already keeps: `POST /v1/cook-sessions/:id/events`. Each event has the app's own sequence number, so a retried request adds nothing twice, and the app's clock in milliseconds: only differences within one session are used, so a phone whose clock is off doesn't matter. The server takes events while the session is under 24 hours old and up to 10 minutes after it finishes, at most 600 a session, and names each step by its key as the recipe has it then. Nothing is kept for the author's own recipes or for a member who keeps their cooking out; the app is never told, and cooking never waits on it.
2. **Questions.** What a cook asks hands-free (`POST /recipes/:id/ask`) or of the sous-chef while on a step in cooking mode is kept with the recipe and step for 90 days (`cook_questions`), unless it's the author's own recipe or the member keeps their cooking out. Keeping it never fails the question.
3. **The nightly pass.** Once a UTC day from 03:00 (checked hourly, so a container that slept catches up, under an advisory lock and a `cook_insight_runs` row, so a second replica or a restart does nothing), the API reads every recipe with something new (`recipe_insights_due`), most saved first. For each it reads its latest 500 sessions with step events in the last 180 days, other cooks' only, and writes per step key (`recipe_step_stats`): how many sessions spent usable time on it, their median and 75th percentile, how many sessions that are over reached it and how many stopped there, and how many started its timer; and for the recipe (`recipe_insights`) how many sessions are over and how many finished. A session still under way keeps its recipe queued for the next night.
4. **Time on a step** is the sum of a session's visits to it, from entering it to entering another step or leaving cooking mode. A visit under 5 seconds is a glance (reading ahead) and doesn't count. A visit longer than the step's timer plus 30 minutes is a phone left running, so that session's time on that step doesn't count at all, and neither does a visit that never ends. Medians keep the rest honest.
5. **Grouping questions.** For the 200 most saved recipes in the pass (`COOK_INSIGHT_QUESTION_RECIPES`), new questions at steps where 3 cooks asked something go to the cheap translation model (GPT-6 Luna through OpenRouter, the newest 40 of a step and 120 of a recipe). It puts each into a question the step already shows or into a new one, reworded so no cook's own words show and answered in a sentence or two from the recipe, with safe temperatures and never a promise about allergies (`ai/questions.go`). What it returns is checked: a question assigned to another step, or anything not asked, is dropped. Questions sent are marked read whatever the answer, so none is paid for twice. A recipe's night typically costs about a tenth of a cent, reserved against the shared budget like translations (`ai_usage` kind `cook_questions`, no member pays); without an OpenRouter key the numbers are still computed and the questions wait.

## Privacy

- **Nobody's own shows.** A step's numbers need 5 sessions, the finish rate 20, and a question 3 cooks asking it; questions show only reworded. Cooks never see where others gave up, only the author does, and also only from 5 sessions.
- **Share how my cooking goes** in My kitchen → **Your cooking** (`preferences.shareCooking`, on until turned off) keeps a member's cooking out. Turning it off also deletes their step events and the questions they asked, and queues those recipes to be read again; their verified cooks stay, since they pay creators and count for cook times.
- **Kept for a while, then gone.** Step events go after 180 days. A session that didn't become a verified cook goes after two days as before, unless it has step events (where cooks give up is part of what the author sees), then after 180 days. Cooks' questions go after 90 days, and a grouped question the sous-chef answered goes 90 days after nobody asks it any more; one its author answered stays. A year of pass records is kept.
- **The member's data.** `GET /v1/me/export` has each session's `stepEvents` and the member's `cookQuestions`, and deleting the account deletes them.

## Thin data and noise

Early on few recipes have 5 cooks. "Early: 3 cooks" shows what there is, and the [creator fund](CREATOR-FUND.md) and the weekly cook-along concentrate cooking on fewer recipes so they turn cook-tested sooner. Phones left running are cut by the idle rule and medians, and taps straight through the steps neither verify a cook nor give a step usable time.

## Verification

`cook_insights_test.go` covers step keys, notes and percentiles, folding sessions into step times (glances, idle phones, giving up), which events are kept and for whom, the pass running once a day from 03:00, what cooks and authors see, editing a step, finish rates from 20 sessions, grouping questions with a stand-in model (paid from the shared budget, never sent twice), the author answering and hiding questions, keeping one's cooking out, the Explore filter and the feed's boost, retention, and questions kept from hands-free cooking and the sous-chef with the step's line reaching the answer.
