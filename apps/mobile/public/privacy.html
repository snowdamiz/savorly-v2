Privacy policy

Last updated 16 October 2026

This policy explains what Savorly collects when you use savorly.cooking and the Savorly apps for iPhone, iPad and Android, why we collect it, who processes it for us, how long we keep it and the choices you have. If you have a question, write to privacy@savorly.cooking.

What we collect

Your account

Your email address, name and username, and your password, which we store only as a one-way hash (bcrypt), never as the password itself. If you add them: a profile photo, a short bio, food preferences such as diets and allergies, your language, measurement units, and which notifications you want.

Signing in with Apple or Google

The account identifier Apple or Google gives us, the email address it shares (Apple may give a private relay address) and, the first time, your name. With Google we start your profile photo from your Google picture. With Apple we also keep a token that we use only to revoke Savorly's access when you delete your account.

What you post and keep

Recipes with their photos and videos, reviews and ratings, reports about recipes, saved recipes and cookbooks, the cooks you follow, meal plans, grocery lists and pantry items. If you start or join a household, we keep its name, its members and who added each item to its shared grocery list.

The sous-chef and other AI features

What you write or say to the sous-chef, photos you show it, the recipe or cooking step on your screen, the results it produces (recipes, answers, images, videos and retouched photos) and records of use that we need for plan allowances and spending limits. Voice conversations are kept as text transcripts; we don't record or keep the audio. Read-aloud audio of recipe steps is generated from the recipe text and shared by everyone who listens to that step.

Payments

When paid plans are offered, Stripe processes payments. We keep your plan, its status and renewal date, and Stripe's identifiers for you and your subscription. We never see or store your card details.

Technical information

Your IP address, which we use to limit how many requests one visitor can make (it isn't stored in our database); sign-in session tokens, which we store only as hashes; and request logs kept for a few days by our hosting provider. If a page's security policy blocks something, your browser may send us a report with the page address and what was blocked.

On your device

On the website, one strictly necessary cookie keeps you signed in, and your browser's local storage remembers your language, unsaved recipe drafts and announcements you dismissed. In the apps, your sign-in is kept in the device's secure storage. We don't use advertising or cross-site tracking cookies.

Notifications

If you allow notifications on your phone, we keep its push token (an address for your device that Apple or Google gives the app) with your account and the sign-in it came from, to send the notifications you choose: new recipes from cooks you follow, reviews and comments on your recipes, and pantry items about to expire. Signing out of the device removes its token, and you can turn each kind off in the app. We keep each notification we send, with its text, for 7 days.

How you use the app

The apps and the website tell us about a few things you do, to show us what to improve: what you search for and how many recipes it found, which recipes you open and from where, when you start cooking a recipe or a sous-chef chat, when you share or import a recipe, and how far you get through setting up your kitchen. Each event carries a random identifier that changes every time you open the app and, if you're signed in, your account. We don't store your IP address or details of your device with them, we keep them for 90 days, and we delete yours with your account. Our administrators see them only as totals, such as the most common searches.

Visits

We may count visits to the website with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which doesn't use cookies, doesn't follow you across sites and reports only totals such as page views.

How we use it

  • To run Savorly: your account and kitchen, publishing and showing recipes, following cooks, and the planner, grocery list and pantry.
  • To provide the features you ask for, including the AI features, for which we send the content each request needs to the provider that does the work (listed below), and the notifications you choose.
  • To understand how Savorly is used, from the app's own events described above, and improve it.
  • To keep Savorly safe: limiting request rates, preventing abuse, and reviewing reports and content. Our administrators can see accounts and content to moderate them.
  • To send the emails you ask for, such as password reset codes.
  • To take payment for paid plans.

We don't sell your personal information, we don't show ads, and we don't use your content to train AI models.

What others can see

Your profile (name, username, photo and bio), your published recipes and reviews, and counts such as followers and saves are public: anyone can see them, including people without an account and search engines, and links to them show a preview. Your email address, drafts, cookbooks, plans, grocery lists, pantry and sous-chef conversations are private to you, except that the members of a household you join see its shared grocery list, who added each item, and your name, username and photo.

Who processes it for us

  • Cloudflare hosts the website and our servers, stores photos and videos, sends our emails and, if we use it, counts visits.
  • Neon hosts our database, in the United States.
  • OpenRouter passes sous-chef, recipe, image and read-aloud requests to the AI models that do the work (currently OpenAI's models and ElevenLabs voices).
  • OpenAI runs live voice conversations: your microphone audio goes straight from your device to OpenAI, which also receives the conversation. We ask OpenAI not to store these sessions.
  • reAPI generates recipe videos from the recipe.
  • Stripe processes payments for paid plans.
  • Apple and Google sign you in when you choose them, and deliver notifications to your devices.
  • Expo delivers updates to the iOS and Android apps, and passes our notifications on to Apple and Google.

These providers handle data under their own terms and privacy policies, and only as needed for what they do for us. Apart from them, we share information only when the law requires it, to protect someone's safety or our rights, or as part of a sale or merger of Savorly, in which case this policy keeps applying to your information.

Where it is processed

Our database is in the United States, and our providers work in the United States and other countries. Where the law requires it, transfers out of your country are covered by safeguards such as standard contractual clauses.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and what you post: until you delete them or your account.
  • Photos and videos you add or generate: deleted from storage about a day after they stop being used, for example when you replace one or delete a recipe; files you upload but never use, two weeks after you upload them; and those you never used about a day after you delete your account. A photo that another cook's adaptation of your recipe still shows stays until it no longer does.
  • Events about how you use the app: 90 days. Notifications we sent: 7 days.
  • Sign-in sessions end after 30 days, or when you sign out.
  • Password reset codes expire after 15 minutes.
  • When you delete your account, we delete it and its content from our database at once. A few things are kept for longer; see deleting your account for what and for how long.

Your choices and rights

  • Change your profile, preferences and language in My kitchen; edit, unpublish or delete your recipes; and delete your account in the app or by email.
  • To get a copy of your information, correct it, have it deleted, or object to how we use it, email privacy@savorly.cooking from the address on your account. We answer within 30 days.
  • Depending on where you live (for example the EU, the UK or California), data protection law gives you these and other rights, including complaining to your data protection authority.

Children

Savorly isn't meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their information. If you think a child has an account, tell us and we will delete it.

Security

Connections are encrypted (HTTPS), passwords and session tokens are stored only as hashes, every private action checks that it is yours, and access to our systems is limited to the people who run Savorly. No service is perfectly secure; if something happens that affects your information, we will tell you as the law requires.

Changes

When this policy changes we update this page and its date, and for significant changes we also tell you in the app or by email.
